Security Tips to Keep Hackers Away

Have I Been Pwned?

The web site Have I Been Pwned? is a website that checks data leaks searching for your email address. A great tool to never forget to keep your passwords fresh and always keep an eye on your data

Do not trust Google + Cookie Stealing Attacks

It is not a new fact that google spies on you, but that is not even what we are talking about today. Have you ever thought of how many time you log in with google? did you ever ask yourself how does that work?

The answer is the same as the reason why a website can log you back in without asking your email and password again: COOKIES!

Since Google saves your login with cookies, if someone, somehow, gets your cookies out of your browser, they can log in your google account, no password required, not even an email. And it is not even that hard. But if someone steals your twitter cookies, it hardly ever will become a significant threat, but your google ones, oh boy you are so damn cooked, you log everywhere using google. Once someone get your google cookies, they have got everything. So as say the investors: "Do not keep all your egg withing the same basket"

So, what can you do? Stop using google will not mean keeping your other cookies safe, the best you can do is never ever opening an http website, since it means your connection is not beeing encrypted, therefore being vulnerable to a man-in-the-middle attack or even the website it self could be a piece in a phishing scheme.

Javascript Threat

Would you let some random programmer, which you do not know, run a code, which you do not know how works under the hood, in your machine, with no bounds?

If you think you would deny such absurd stuff, why do you allow pages to run Javascript/Typescript codes directly from your browser in your machine?

Many attacks, to be fair, most of them, rely heavily on JS to run mallicious commands in your browser, for instance, to extract cookies which refer to other domains, like the previous topic with google, and even allocate mallicious bytes directly in your RAM, which could be bytes of a program which will be executed and log all you key presses.

Obviously, not all websites with Javascript are mallicious, many of them just need it to use an API or to load you interactions, like how would youtube send a ginal to their databases from your client side telling the server that you had liked the video with other but javascript.

Disabling Javascript may broke many sites, you not even have to do it, being aware is a great start, but if you go for it, you do not have to go full schizo and never access any website with javascript in your lifetime, the most practical tool is a browser extension called No Script which blocks javascript per Script tag in a html, for instance if the website refers to 5 file .js in their html head, you can unblock only the ones which you trust, it is pretty common to see websites trying to run things that have nothing to do with their goal, many times thing related to advertising, but know you can cut the weed from its root.

With no script you can also set a list of trusted webstites, keep that in mind ;) .

VPN and Network Tunneling

Remember the cookie stealing stuff? I mentioned man-in-the-middle briefly, and man-in-the-middle-attack are not that hard to do, a bad-intentioned agent in the same network as you could watch all of your traffic if it is unencrypted, and if they are a bit more capable, they could do it remotely.

As the paragraph set explicit, fot the attack to work it is necessary to the traffic to unencrypted, so the clear solution is to not depend in the website encryption but to encrypt the traffic in the client side.

There is just one matter on it, a Virtual Private Network can tunnel your traffic through an encrypted connection but it can't prevent the server to do mallicious thing once the data arrives at their side.

Using a VPN cannot make the internet stupidity-proof. Watch your way.

Virus Total

I guess this is kinda of a known one, but here we go.

Downloading files from the internet was never considered a safe activity by any user. But now virustotal have been on the scene for many years.

For the ones who do not know virustotal is a website to which you can upload files, or enter url to verify if there is any trace of mallicious behavior on that. A pretty straightfoward idea. It tests the subject with dozens of antiviruses and flags if one of them give signs of a posssible malware.

Aliases

Back to Have I Been Pwned, I said it works on leaks linked to an email address, well aliases are tools which the goal is to protect your email, at least your real one.

The core idea is the aliases act as an email, you can create accounts on platforms using it, but there is no email client no inbox, all the emails sent to the aliases will just be fowarded to the email you set it to, so in case of leak, one the alias will be compromised, but not your real email address, you can create one for each service you use, if your goal is solely to avoid leaks.

I say "solely" because that is not the only use people do of aliases, many people use aliases for hiding their identity, since the same email address can be tracked across websites, and this point is even more appealing with other kinds of aliases, like phone number aliases, which will redirect all th messages and calls they receive to your real phone number while keeping your day-a-day phone number private. There are aliases for almost any kind of personal information, like phone numbers, emails, credit cards, even real world addresses. If you care about your data you should check it out.
